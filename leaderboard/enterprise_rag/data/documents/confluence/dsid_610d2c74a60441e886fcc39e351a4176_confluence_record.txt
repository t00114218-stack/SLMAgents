Enterprise Record: Our Key Management Standard says we must not cache plaintext

Document ID: dsid_610d2c74a60441e886fcc39e351a4176
System Source: confluence
Details & Summary:
Follow the Key Management Standard's intent: never persist or long-term cache plaintext DEKs. The only permitted "cache" is the ADR-007 constrained optimization: short-lived, in-process-only reuse of plaintext DEKs that are already in memory for request handling (bounded TTL/size, no disk/DB/distributed cache, disable-able in regulated environments). In customer-facing rotation/outage materials, describe it as: Redwood uses envelope encryption; DEKs are wrapped by the customer's KEK in KMS/HSM; Redwood may retain short-lived in-memory cryptographic material for active workloads but does not rely on indefinite caching of KMS responses. If KMS is unavailable/degraded, operations that require wrap/unwrap may fail fast with explicit errors; Redwood uses timeouts, bounded retries with backoff/jitter, circuit breaker/backpressure to prevent retry storms. Do not promise continued decrypt during a KMS outage or disclose exact timeout/retry/breaker thresholds. During rotation, mention dual-decrypt/rollback, and that we do not fan out across keys on timeout/throttling-only bounded fallback for key-mismatch/InvalidCiphertext-type cases.

- Policy should follow the Key Management Standard intent: never persist or long term cache plaintext DEKs.
- The only permitted DEK caching is the ADR-007 constrained optimization: short lived, in process only reuse of plaintext DEKs already in memory for request handling.
- Permitted DEK reuse must be bounded by TTL and cache size, must not use disk, database, or distributed caches, and must be disableable in regulated environments.
- Customer facing materials should state that Redwood uses envelope encryption and that DEKs are wrapped by the customers KEK in KMS or an HSM.
- Customer facing materials should state that Redwood may retain short lived in memory cryptographic material for active workloads, but does not rely on indefinite caching of KMS responses.
- Customer facing materials should state that if KMS is unavailable or degraded, operations requiring wrap or unwrap may fail fast with explicit errors.
- Customer facing materials should state that Redwood uses timeouts, bounded retries with backoff and jitter, and circuit breaker or backpressure to prevent retry storms during KMS issues.
- Customer facing materials should not promise continued decrypt during a KMS outage and should not disclose exact timeout, retry, or circuit breaker thresholds.
- Rotation materials should mention dual decrypt and rollback behavior.
- Rotation materials should state that on timeout or throttling Redwood does not fan out across keys, and only performs bounded fallback for key mismatch or InvalidCiphertext type cases.

Additional Enterprise Context: Verified in production cluster telemetry and internal system documentation.
