Details & Summary:
To ship an emergency **serving-runtime hotfix** to production (Hosted + Dedicated), Redwood Inference follows this end-to-end process:

1) **Open the emergency change + record the minimum checklist** (in a Linear/Jira ticket and the incident timeline/thread): state the fix hypothesis, what's changing (code/config/infra), blast radius (Hosted regions, Dedicated clusters/customers, model families), risk assessment (security/data/perf), a **rollback plan with explicit triggers**, and who owns post-change monitoring; ensure an incident channel/thread exists with an identified IC.

2) **Get required approvals (no exceptions):** two-person approval from **(a) IC (or SRE on-call)** and **(b) the serving-runtime Service Owner (or delegate)**, recorded in PR review/incident thread/change record. Add scenario-based approvals as applicable: **Security on-call** (security/exploit), **Product on-call/PM** (customer-visible behavior changes), and **Customer Success Escalation Manager (CSEM)** when Dedicated/customer maintenance coordination is required.

3) **Prepare the hotfix PR (Serving Runtime Hotfix Runbook):** cut `hotfix/runtime-<YYYYMMDD>-<short-issue>`, cherry-pick only the minimal fix, update the Runtime Hotfixes changelog, open a PR (prefer release branch), and obtain emergency reviews from **runtime on-call + SRE on-call (or IC)**; include incident link, risk, rollback, and rollout owners.

4) **Build + supply-chain sign artifacts (mandatory):** run the approved hotfix build pipeline to produce the new runtime image in the production registry plus **SBOM** and **provenance attestation**, and **verify cosign signatures/attestations before rollout**; image signing cannot be skipped (SBOM delay is only allowed with Security on-call approval and must be attached within 4 hours).

5) **Validate before rollout (minimum required even in Sev-1):** run unit tests for the affected module, one perf canary for a representative model, and a stress test matching the incident pattern; record results in the PR confirming error-rate improvement, **no >10% p95 regression** (same workload), and no increase in OOM/GPU reset counters.

6) **Post rollout notes to the incident channel:** new runtime image tag, expected rollout impact (restarts/brief resets), the **canary target**, and **rollback criteria** (metrics + customer symptom). A canary is required unless explicitly waived by IC (with rationale recorded).

7) **Roll out to Hosted (Platform/SRE; Hosted Rollout + Rollback Procedure):** announce rollout start + tag; set `runtime_image_tag=<new-tag>` and sync via ArgoCD (`hosted-runtime`). Roll out in order **us-west-2a canary (10%) → us-west-2 (50%) → us-east-1 (50%) → eu-central-1 (50%) → global**, monitoring each step for at least 15 minutes (success rate, p95 for top routes, GPU OOM, router fallback, error-budget burn). **Rollback immediately** by reverting to the last-known-good tag and ArgoCD syncing if 5xx/stream aborts persist >5 min, OOM spikes >2× baseline, p95 regresses >20% for >10 min, crash loops appear, or customer reports correlate with rollout start; announce rollback and next steps in the incident channel.

8) **Roll out to Dedicated (Infra + CSEM; Dedicated Cluster Hotfix Rollout Notes):** collect required inputs (customer/cluster ID, current/target tags, expected restart impact, rollback tag, scope), obtain approvals (**IC/SRE + Dedicated service owner + CSEM sign-off on messaging**), and **communicate to customers** (unless IC explicitly waives due to active outage): state brief interruption risk, provide a rollout window, and describe rollback criteria in plain language. Canary one Dedicated cluster first (prefer internal dogfood/lowest-traffic with CSEM approval) and validate ~30 minutes (success rate, p95, OOM, restart frequency, customer health checks); then schedule remaining clusters, rolling **no more than 3 in parallel unless IC approves**. Per cluster: confirm comms sent, update Helm `runtime_image_tag`, ArgoCD sync `dedicated-<cluster-id>-runtime`, watch for CrashLoopBackOff, run customer smoke checks (chat/embeddings/streaming). If rollback triggers hit, revert the tag, restart if needed, and **notify the customer immediately** (CSEM).

9) **Mandatory Sev-1 customer communications (Status Page + Customer Notifications):** IC owns accuracy/cadence; CSEM owns direct enterprise/Dedicated outreach; initial acknowledgment **within 10 minutes**, updates **every 30 minutes** while impact continues, and a resolution message **within 15 minutes** of confirmed mitigation. Each update must include customer symptoms, affected surfaces (regions/clusters/models), mitigation steps, ETA with confidence level, and any workarounds; provide a post-incident summary within **1 business day** (required for enterprise).

10) **Post-change closeout:** update the incident timeline with start/change/outcome, record cluster/region outcomes, and within **2 business days** complete incident follow-up (what changed and why it worked), document any release-process deviations/corrective actions, remove temporary flags/throttles (with owner + deadline), and create a task to promote the hotfix into the next regular release.

- An emergency serving-runtime hotfix requires opening an emergency change ticket and recording a minimum checklist: fix hypothesis, what is changing (code/config/infra), blast radius (Hosted regions and Dedicated clusters/customers), risk assessment (security/data/perf), an explicit rollback plan with triggers, and a named owner for post-change monitoring, with an incident channel/thread and identified incident commander (IC).
- Required approvals for an emergency serving-runtime hotfix are two-person approval from (a) the IC or SRE on-call and (b) the serving-runtime Service Owner (or delegate), recorded in the PR review or incident thread/change record.
- Additional scenario-based approvals apply: Security on-call for security or exploit risk, Product on-call or PM for customer-visible behavior changes, and a Customer Success Escalation Manager (CSEM) when Dedicated or customer maintenance coordination is required.
- The hotfix PR process includes cutting a hotfix branch, cherry-picking only the minimal fix, updating the runtime hotfixes changelog, and opening a PR (prefer the release branch) that includes incident link, risk, rollback plan, and rollout owners, with emergency reviews from runtime on-call and SRE on-call (or IC).
- Before rollout, the hotfix build must produce a new runtime image in the production registry along with SBOM and provenance attestation, and cosign signatures/attestations must be verified; image signing cannot be skipped, and any SBOM delay requires Security on-call approval and must be attached within 4 hours.
- Minimum pre-rollout validation is required even in Sev-1: unit tests for the affected module, a performance canary for a representative model, and a stress test matching the incident pattern, with results recorded (including error-rate improvement, no more than 10 percent p95 regression on the same workload, and no increase in OOM/GPU reset counters).
- Before executing rollout steps, the incident channel must be updated with the new runtime image tag, expected rollout impact (restarts/brief resets), the canary target, and rollback criteria; a canary is required unless explicitly waived by the IC with rationale recorded.
- Hosted rollout is executed by Platform/SRE by setting runtime_image_tag to the new tag and syncing via ArgoCD for hosted-runtime, with rollout in order: us-west-2a canary (10 percent), then us-west-2 (50 percent), us-east-1 (50 percent), eu-central-1 (50 percent), then global.
- Hosted rollout requires monitoring each step for at least 15 minutes using metrics including success rate, p95 for top routes, GPU OOM, router fallback, and error-budget burn.
- Hosted rollback is performed by reverting to the last-known-good tag and ArgoCD syncing if any rollback trigger occurs, and the rollback plus next steps must be announced in the incident channel.
- Hosted rollback triggers include: 5xx or stream aborts persisting more than 5 minutes, OOM spikes more than 2x baseline, p95 latency regressing more than 20 percent for more than 10 minutes, crash loops, or customer reports correlating with rollout start.
- Dedicated rollout is executed by Infra with CSEM involvement, after collecting required inputs (customer/cluster ID, current/target tags, expected restart impact, rollback tag, and scope) and obtaining approvals from IC/SRE, the Dedicated service owner, and CSEM sign-off on customer messaging.
- For Dedicated, customers must be communicated to (unless the IC explicitly waives due to active outage), including brief interruption risk, a rollout window, and rollback criteria in plain language.
- Dedicated rollout must canary one cluster first (prefer internal dogfood or lowest-traffic with CSEM approval) and validate for about 30 minutes using metrics including success rate, p95, OOM, restart frequency, and customer health checks.
- After Dedicated canary validation, remaining clusters are scheduled with no more than 3 clusters in parallel unless the IC approves more.
- Per Dedicated cluster, execution includes confirming customer communications were sent, updating Helm runtime_image_tag, ArgoCD syncing dedicated-<cluster-id>-runtime, monitoring for CrashLoopBackOff, and running customer smoke checks (chat/embeddings/streaming).
- If Dedicated rollback triggers are hit, the tag is reverted (and restarted if needed) and the customer must be notified immediately by CSEM.
- Mandatory Sev-1 customer communications include use of a Status Page and customer notifications, with the IC owning accuracy and cadence and the CSEM owning direct enterprise/Dedicated outreach.
- Sev-1 communications timing requirements are: initial acknowledgment within 10 minutes, updates every 30 minutes while impact continues, and a resolution message within 15 minutes of confirmed mitigation.
- Each Sev-1 customer update must include customer symptoms, affected surfaces (regions/clusters/models), mitigation steps, ETA with confidence level, and any workarounds.
- A post-incident summary is required within 1 business day for enterprise customers.
- Post-change closeout includes updating the incident timeline with start/change/outcome and recording region/cluster outcomes, completing incident follow-up within 2 business days (what changed and why it worked), documenting release-process deviations and corrective actions, removing temporary flags/throttles with an owner and deadline, and creating a task to promote the hotfix into the next regular release.
