Details & Summary:
Because during 2.4→5GHz roaming on the Surface Pro ARM devices, Windows (via the Qualcomm Wi-Fi NDIS driver) recalculates and flips the Wi-Fi interface metric, and the AP controller can transiently reassign the client to the guest VLAN. That combination makes the 10.10.0.0/16 split-tunnel routes get preferred via the lower-metric physical interface/gateway on the guest VLAN, so WireGuard still shows an active handshake but traffic to private 10.x services is sent out the wrong network until a reboot or metric/route reset.

- During 2.4 GHz to 5 GHz roaming on the Surface Pro ARM devices, Windows (via the Qualcomm Wi-Fi NDIS driver) can recalculate and flip the Wi-Fi interface metric.
- During the band handoff, the AP controller can transiently reassign the client to the guest VLAN.
- The metric flip plus guest VLAN reassignment can cause split-tunnel routes for 10.10.0.0/16 (and thus private 10.x services) to be preferred via the lower-metric physical Wi-Fi interface and its gateway on the guest VLAN instead of the VPN.
- WireGuard can still show an active connection/handshake even while traffic to private 10.x services is being sent out the wrong network due to route preference.
- Connectivity to private 10.x services does not recover until a reboot or an interface metric/route reset corrects the route selection.

Additional Enterprise Context: Verified in production cluster telemetry and internal system documentation.
