The staircase of 502/503s was triggered by a burst of long-lived streaming chat connections plus a bulk embedding job, which caused the ephemeral prewarm system to spin up warmers that made many short-lived TLS handshakes. Concurrent autoscaler scale-in churned those warmers, creating repeated handshake timeouts/SYN backlog growth and accept-queue backpressure, while KV-cache checkpointing amplified small NVMe writes (iowait), leading to worker delays/evictions and upstream resets that the gateway surfaced as sustained failures.

- The multi-hour staircase of gateway 502/503 failures was triggered by long-lived streaming chat connections coinciding with a bulk embedding job during peak traffic.
- The ephemeral prewarm system spun up warmers that performed many short-lived TLS handshakes.
- Autoscaler scale-in churn repeatedly terminated and recreated those warmers, increasing warmup connection churn.
- The warmup churn led to repeated TLS handshake timeouts, SYN backlog growth, and accept-queue backpressure.
- Disk-backed KV-cache checkpointing amplified small NVMe writes, increasing iowait and write pressure.
- The resulting iowait and backpressure caused worker delays and evictions and upstream resets, which the gateway surfaced as sustained failures.
